# References

This file is the audit trail for the optimization work on this engine: it records
the sources behind the techniques the engine uses or has evaluated. Entries were
compiled from the three reconnaissance artifacts (`agent://LitMoEOffload`,
`agent://LitLowBitKernels`, `agent://LitMemEfficient`); titles, author lists,
years, venues, URLs and measured numbers are reproduced as those artifacts report
them, and nothing is added or merged. An entry marked **[UNVERIFIED]**, or one the
artifact describes as seen only in a secondary summary, was not confirmed at the
primary source.

## Evaluated but not used

- **Expert pruning (REAP as pruning).** Sources: REAP (2025); Jaiswal et al. (2025);
  MoE-Pruner (2024); Not All Experts are Equal (2024); EEP (2024); the merge/decompress
  follow-ups MC-SMoE (2024), HC-SMoE (2025), D²-MoE (2025), DiEP (2025), Sub-MoE (2026),
  PuzzleMoE (2026). Reason: accuracy — the engine keeps every router-selected expert and
  never prunes or merges; `docs/decoding-and-memory.md` states the router and weights are
  not pruned, reweighted or requantized, and REAP's saliency is borrowed only as a cache
  priority.
- **Fused LUT matvec kernels.** Sources: LUT-GEMM; T-MAC; llama.cpp Metal
  `mul_mv.metal` IQ kernels; llama.cpp Discussion #5617; Benazir & Lin (2025). Reason:
  memory/throughput on this hardware — Apple GPUs punish codebook indirection (IQ2_XS
  ≈50 t/s vs Q4_0 63 t/s on a 30-core M2 Max), the measured decode is already ~165 GiB/s
  of packed input, so the pending win is fusing dequant into the matmul rather than a
  portable LUT matvec.
- **Host/CPU expert execution.** Sources: Fiddler (2025); PowerInfer (2024);
  PowerInfer-2 (2024); T-MAC; ZeRO-Inference (2022). Reason: memory — on this
  unified-memory machine CPU and GPU share one pool, so there is no host→device weight
  transfer to avoid; experts stream from SSD instead, and CPU compute would contend for
  the same bandwidth.
- **Layer-level weight streaming.** Sources: FlexGen (2023); LLM in a flash (2024);
  `matt-k-wong/mlx-flash` manager; Colibri expert store. Reason: memory/design fit —
  dense weights are touched every token and stay resident; only routed experts stream
  (`docs/decoding-and-memory.md` records why layer-level streaming was not adopted).
- **Speculative decoding / multi-token prediction.** Sources: Leviathan et al. (2023);
  Chen et al. (2023); Medusa (2024); EAGLE (2024); Lookahead (2024); MagicDec (2024);
  DeepSeek-V3 MTP (2024); mlx-dspark. Reason: not yet validated — installed speculative
  generation requires every target cache to be trimmable and Qwen's `ArraysCache` fails
  that check, native MTP weights are stripped by the pinned MLX-LM sanitization, and a
  wider verify block can activate more unique experts and increase I/O
  (`docs/decoding-and-memory.md`).
- **KV eviction schemes.** Sources: H2O (2023); StreamingLLM (2024); TOVA (2024);
  SnapKV (2024); PyramidKV (2024); Quest (2024); ShadowKV (2025); CacheGen (2024);
  PagedAttention/vLLM (2023); Mooncake (2024). Reason: not yet validated — the engine
  serves a uniform 4-bit KV cache with inactive-KV persistence, and
  `docs/decoding-and-memory.md` records that no active-request token-level KV paging or
  eviction is implemented.

### Expert offloading systems

Source artifact: `agent://LitMoEOffload`, headings "A. Expert offloading & caching systems
(weights > memory)" and "D. Dynamic expert placement between accelerator and host memory".

- **MoE-Infinity: Efficient MoE Inference on Personal Machines with Sparsity-Aware Expert Cache** — Leyang Xue, Yao Fu, Zhan Lu, Luo Mai, Mahesh Marina (2024, v3 Mar 2025), arXiv:2401.14361. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14361 (code: https://github.com/EfficientMoE/MoE-Infinity) — batch-1 personal-machine serving; records per-request expert-activation traces at request level, derives a sparsity pattern, and uses it to drive expert-cache replacement and prefetching while offloading expert weights to host RAM/SSD — reported: 3.1–16.7x per-token latency improvement over vLLM, Ollama, DeepSpeed and BrainStorm on DeepSeek/Mixtral. Short version titled *MoE-Infinity: Activation-Aware Expert Offloading for Efficient MoE Serving* appeared at the UK Systems Workshop 2024 (https://uksystems.org/workshop/2024/papers/srcw24-paper13.pdf). **[UNVERIFIED: a EuroSys'25 attribution circulates but MoE-Infinity does not appear in the EuroSys 2025 accepted-papers list; treat venue as preprint/workshop.]**
- **DeepSpeed-MoE: Advancing Mixture-of-Experts Inference and Training to Power Next-Generation AI Scale** — Samyam Rajbhandari, Conglong Li, Zhewei Yao, Minjia Zhang, Reza Yazdani Aminabadi, Ammar Ahmad Awan, Jeff Rasley, Yuxiong He (2022), ICML 2022 (PMLR v162). https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05596 ; https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/rajbhandari22a.html — expert parallelism + tensor slicing + PR-MoE (pyramid-residual) and model compression to cut per-GPU expert footprint — reported: up to 3.7x MoE model-size reduction; 7.3x better latency/cost than prior MoE inference; up to 4.5x faster / 9x cheaper than quality-equivalent dense. Tutorial: https://www.deepspeed.ai/tutorials/mixture-of-experts-inference/
- **ZeRO-Inference: Democratizing massive model inference** — DeepSpeed team (2022), DeepSpeed blog, 9 Sep 2022. https://www.deepspeed.ai/2022/09/09/zero-inference.html (code: https://github.com/deepspeedai/DeepSpeedExamples/blob/master/inference/huggingface/zero_inference/README.md) — keeps all weights outside the GPU in a GPU→CPU→NVMe hierarchy via ZeRO-3 `offload_param` — reported: full offload ~43 tok/s from CPU RAM and ~30 tok/s from NVMe, ~1.3x / ~2.4x better than partial offload.
- **Fast Inference of Mixture-of-Experts Language Models with Offloading** — Artyom Eliseev, Denis Mazur (2023), arXiv:2312.17238. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17238 (code: https://github.com/dvmazur/mixtral-offloading) — mixed HQQ quantization (different schemes for attention vs experts), per-expert offloading with an LRU expert cache, plus speculative expert prefetching — reported: runs Mixtral-8x7B on free-tier Colab T4 / 12–16 GB consumer GPUs.
- **Fiddler: CPU-GPU Orchestration for Fast Inference of Mixture-of-Experts Models** — Keisuke Kamahori, Tian Tang, Yile Gu, Kan Zhu, Baris Kasikci (2025), ICLR 2025, arXiv:2402.07033. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07033 (code: https://github.com/efeslab/fiddler) — keeps all experts in CPU RAM and moves the small activation GPU→CPU and back instead of the large weight tensors, choosing per layer between GPU compute, weight transfer, and CPU execution — reported: 1.26x single-batch, 1.30x long-prefill, 11.57x beam-search speedup vs prior systems.
- **Pre-gated MoE: An Algorithm-System Co-Design for Fast and Scalable Mixture-of-Expert Inference** — Ranggi Hwang, Jianyu Wei, Shijie Cao, Changho Hwang, Xiaohu Tang, Ting Cao, Mao Yang (2024), ISCA 2024, arXiv:2308.12066. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12066 (DOI 10.1109/ISCA59077.2024.00078; code: https://github.com/ranggihwang/Pregated_MoE) — a pre-gating function predicts which experts a layer will need before the expensive gating stage, so non-selected expert parameters stay off-GPU — reported: only ~23% latency overhead vs oracular all-on-GPU execution (~81% of ideal throughput); ~1.5x faster than on-demand loading and ~27x faster than M-prefetch; peak GPU memory cut to roughly a quarter of the GPU-only baseline (~4.2x reduction) [memory figure from secondary summary — see note].
- **SiDA-MoE: Sparsity-Inspired Data-Aware Serving for Efficient and Scalable Large Mixture-of-Experts Models** — Zhixu Du, Shiyu Li, Yuhao Wu, Xiangyu Jiang, Jingwei Sun, Qilin Zheng, Yongkai Wu, Ang Li, Hai "Helen" Li, Yiran Chen (2024), MLSys 2024, arXiv:2310.18859. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18859 (https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/698cfaf72a208aef2e78bcac55b74328-Abstract-Conference.html) — an offline-trained hash function replaces the router so the exact expert set is known ahead of time; inactive experts are pre-offloaded to system RAM — reported: up to 3.93x throughput, up to 72% latency reduction, up to 80% GPU memory saving, <1% performance drop.
- **HOBBIT: A Mixed Precision Expert Offloading System for Fast MoE Inference** — Zhiyuan Tang et al. (2024), arXiv:2411.01433. https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01433 — replaces cache-miss experts with low-precision versions kept on the GPU, combined with mixed-precision-aware prefetching and a sequence-level, multidimensional expert caching policy — reported: up to 9.93x token-level decoding speedup; 3.21x over MoE-Offloading on Mixtral-8x7B (3.29x Phi-MoE), 2.30x/3.92x over MoE-Infinity; <1% top-1 accuracy drop. **[UNVERIFIED venue: secondary pages claim ICLR 2025 and ASPLOS 2025; only the arXiv record is confirmed.]**
- **MoE-Lightning: High-Throughput MoE Inference on Memory-constrained GPUs** — Shiyi Cao, Shu Liu, Tyler Griggs, Peter Schafhalter, Xiaoxuan Liu, Ying Sheng, Joseph E. - **MoE-Gen: High-Throughput MoE Inference on a Single GPU with Module-Based Batching** — Tairan Xu, Leyang Xue, Zhan Lu, Adrian Jackson, Luo Mai (2025), arXiv:2503.09716. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09716 (code: https://github.com/EfficientMoE/MoE-Gen) — module-based batching: tokens accumulate in host memory and are launched in large per-module batches so each expert is invoked once per step, with batch sizes chosen to overlap compute and transfer — reported: 8–31x higher throughput than FlexGen/MoE-Lightning/DeepSpeed on DeepSeek and Mixtral.
- **ExpertFlow: Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Inference via Predictive Expert Caching and Token Scheduling** — Xin He et al. - **Taming Latency-Memory Trade-Off in MoE-Based LLM Serving via Fine-Grained Expert Offloading (FineMoE)** — Hanfei Yu, Xingqi Cui, Hong Zhang, Hao Wang, Hao Wang (2026), EuroSys 2026, arXiv:2502.05370. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05370 (https://2026.eurosys.org/papers.html) — per-expert prefetch/cache/offload decisions driven by an "expert map": iteration-level gate probability distributions plus semantic hints from the prompt — reported: 47% lower inference latency and +39% expert hit rate over SOTA; the EuroSys version additionally reports up to 36% TPOT reduction vs DeepSpeed-Inference on a 6 GB GPU (secondary source).
- **SwapMoE: Serving Off-the-shelf MoE-based Large Language Models with Tunable Memory Budget** — Rui Kong, Yuanchun Li, Qingtian Feng, Weijun Wang, Xiaozhou Ye, Ye Ouyang, Linghe Kong, Yunxin Liu (2024), ACL 2024, arXiv:2308.15030. https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15030 — keeps a small tunable set of "virtual experts" resident and maintains a runtime mapping from virtual to real experts — reported: 14.2 GiB → 4.7 GiB memory on Switch Transformer summarization, 50% latency reduction, −0.041 ROUGE-2.

- **FlashMoE: Reducing SSD I/O Bottlenecks via ML-Based Cache Replacement for Mixture-of-Experts Inference on Edge Devices** — Byeongju Kim, Jungwan Lee, Donghyeon Han, Hoi-Jun Yoo, Sangyeob Kim (2026), arXiv:2601.17063. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17063 — offloads inactive experts to SSD (not DRAM) and uses a lightweight learned cache-replacement policy that mixes recency and frequency signals to maximize expert reuse — reported: up to +51% cache hit rate over LRU/LFU and up to 2.6x speedup over existing MoE inference systems on a user-grade desktop platform.
- **MoE-SpeQ: Speculative Quantized Decoding with Proactive Expert Prefetching and Offloading for Mixture-of-Experts** — Wenfeng Wang, Jiacheng Liu, Xiaofeng Hou, Xinfeng Xia, Peng Tang, Mingxuan Zhang, Chao Li, Minyi Guo (2025), arXiv:2511.14102. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14102 — a small on-device draft model predicts the expert sequence for future tokens so an orchestrator can prefetch from host memory and hide PCIe latency; an adaptive governor follows an Amortization Roofline Model — reported: up to 2.34x speedup over the SOTA offloading framework on Phi-MoE.

### Expert cache policy and prefetching

Source artifact: `agent://LitMoEOffload`, heading "B. Expert caching policies and
router-prediction prefetching".

- **ProMoE: Fast MoE-based LLM Serving using Proactive Caching** — Xiaoniu Song, Zihang Zhong, Rong Chen, Haibo Chen (2024, v3 2025), arXiv:2410.22134. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22134 — uses intermediate results already computed in the current layer to predict subsequent expert usage and fetch proactively, taking loading off the critical path instead of reacting to cache misses — reported: 2.20x average (up to 3.21x) prefill speedup and 2.07x average (up to 5.02x) decode speedup over existing offloading solutions.

- **SpecPrefetch: Parameter-Efficient Expert Prefetching for Sparse MoE Foundation Models** — Jinwei Kong, Runqi Meng, Fanyi Wang, Wentao Qiu, Haotian Hu, Yongjian Zhou, Zhenhua Ge (2026), arXiv:2607.24787. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24787 (code: https://github.com/wei390/SpecPrefetch) — a shared lightweight adapter predicts next-layer expert candidates only for asynchronous transfer; the frozen native router still decides execution, so prediction errors cost bandwidth, not accuracy; a window-aware scheduler prioritizes transfers under cache/bandwidth limits — reported: best average expert recall in 9/10 settings (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, DeepSeek-VL2-Tiny); up to +20% decoding throughput on a Snapdragon 8 Elite over a compute-optimized offloading runtime.
- **Accurate Expert Predictions in MoE Inference via Cross-Layer Gate** — authors not recorded in the artifact (2025), arXiv:2502.12224. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12224 — uses cross-layer gate signals to predict expert activation more accurately than single-layer lookahead. *(Abstract-level metadata only; no numbers extracted here.)* **[partially verified]**
- **DuoServe-MoE: Dual-Phase Expert Prefetch and Cache Scheduling for Efficient MoE LLM Inference** — authors not recorded in the artifact (2025), arXiv:2509.07379. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07379 — separates prefill and decode phases and applies distinct prefetch/cache heuristics to each. *(Abstract-level metadata only; no numbers extracted here.)* **[partially verified]**
- **OD-MoE: On-Demand Expert Loading for Cacheless Edge-Distributed MoE Inference** — authors not recorded in the artifact (2025), arXiv:2512.03927. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03927 — loads each target expert just-in-time to a distributed node and evicts immediately, avoiding a persistent cache. *(Seen via search summary only.)* **[UNVERIFIED]**

- **REAP the Experts: Why Pruning Prevails for One-Shot MoE Compression** — Mike Lasby, Ivan Lazarevich, Nish Sinnadurai, Sean Lie, Yani Ioannou, Vithursan Thangarasa (Cerebras Systems; Univ. of Calgary) (2025, v3 May 2026), arXiv:2510.13999. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13999 (code: https://github.com/CerebrasResearch/reap; blog: https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/reap; vLLM/LLM-Compressor port: https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/llm-compressor/en/latest/examples/reap_expert_pruning) — saliency (Eq. 9): for expert *j*, `S_j = (1/|X_j|) · Σ_{x∈X_j} g_j(x) · ‖f_j(x)‖₂`, where `X_j = {x | j ∈ T(x)}` is the set of tokens that route to expert *j*, `g_j(x)` is the router gate value and `f_j(x)` the expert output; the average is conditional over tokens where the expert is active, decoupling functional impact from activation frequency and minimizing the upper bound on the pruning substitution error `g_j(x)(‖f_j(x)‖ + ‖f_i(x)‖)`; the paper also proves merging with summed gates incurs an irreducible error ∝ `E[(g_i+g_j)²]·Var[r(x)]·G_ij` — reported (Tables 2–3): Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B at 50% expert pruning non-agentic coding 0.619 vs 0.636 baseline (≈1.2 points; Δacc ≤ 2% claimed in the abstract), EvalPlus 0.822 vs 0.841, LiveCodeBench 0.415 vs 0.431; at 25% pruning the mean coding drop is 0.16%; Kimi-K2-Instruct (W4A16) at 50% coding 0.624 vs 0.631, at 25% 0.640 (above baseline), SWE-Bench-Verified 0.580 vs 0.554; excluding large-scale models, mean coding drop 1.9% @25% and 6.9% @50% vs >5% @25% and >20% @50% for HC-SMoE/M-SMoE merging, math mean drop 0.1% @25%; on multiple-choice it concedes to merging (≈13% mean drop at 50% vs HC-SMoE ≈4%). ### Speculative decoding

Source artifact: `agent://LitMemEfficient`, heading "5. Speculative decoding /
